React toasts are lightweight notifications designed to mimic the push notifications that have been popularized by mobile and desktop operating systems. They're built with flexbox, so they're easy to align and position.

## Overview

Things to know when using the toast plugin:

- Toasts are opt-in — nothing renders until you flip `visible` to `true` directly, or call `addToast()` to push one into a `Toaster`'s queue.
- Toasts will automatically hide after `delay` milliseconds unless you set `autohide={false}`. The countdown pauses while the pointer is over the toast or focus is within it, and only resumes once both leave.
- Use `role="status"` (the default) for routine confirmations and informational updates. Reserve `role="alert"` for errors and messages requiring immediate attention. Both roles carry implicit live-region semantics, so no separate `aria-live` prop is needed.
- The `image`/`title`/`time`/`message`/`footer`/`closeButton` props are shorthand for the manual `ToastHeader`/`ToastBody`/`ToastFooter` composition — reach for manual composition only when a toast needs more than one header/body/footer block. See [Shorthand props](#shorthand-props).

## Examples

### Basic

To encourage extensible and predictable toasts, we recommend a header and body. Toast headers use `display: flex`, allowing easy alignment of content thanks to our margin and flexbox utilities.

Toasts are as flexible as you need and have very little required markup. At a minimum, we require a single element to contain your "toasted" content and strongly encourage a dismiss button.

For dynamic notifications, call `addToast()` from anywhere — an event handler, an async callback — to push a toast into a `Toaster`'s queue. `Toaster` renders whatever's currently queued; nothing else needs to know about it.

### Shorthand props

For the common case — an image, a heading, a timestamp, one block of message text — `image`/`title`/`time`/`message` render the same markup as composing `ToastHeader`/`ToastBody` by hand, without the wiring. Add `footer` for a single row of trailing content (shorthand for `ToastFooter`), and `closeButton`/`closeLabel` for the dismiss control and its accessible name. When both `title` and `message` are set, the toast's `aria-labelledby`/`aria-describedby` are wired to them automatically. `image` is hidden from assistive technology by default, since it duplicates `title` visually.

  <ShorthandExample client:load />

`image`/`title`/`time`/`message`/`footer` are additive with `children` — for multi-block content (more than one header, body, or footer region), compose `ToastHeader`/`ToastBody`/`ToastFooter` manually instead, as in the sections below.
